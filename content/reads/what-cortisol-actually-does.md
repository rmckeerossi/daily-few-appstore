Cortisol is often called the stress hormone, and it has a rough reputation online. In reality, you couldn’t get through a day without it.

## Its daily job

Cortisol is made by the adrenal glands, which sit on top of your kidneys. It follows a rhythm: it rises sharply in the first hour after you wake, helping you feel alert, then falls through the day to its lowest point around bedtime.

Along the way it helps:

- Release energy, by raising blood sugar when you need it
- Keep blood pressure steady
- Calm inflammation
- Shape when you feel awake and when you feel sleepy

## When stress keeps it going

In a short burst of stress, cortisol rises, helps you respond, then settles. With ongoing stress, poor sleep or irregular routines, that rhythm can flatten or shift later. That can look like:

- Feeling groggy in the morning and alert at night
- Stronger cravings, especially for sweet or rich foods
- Lighter, more broken sleep
- Changes to your cycle

## A note on testing

You may see saliva or urine cortisol tests sold online. Cortisol changes hour to hour, so a single result is hard to interpret. Doctors use specific tests when they suspect a true adrenal condition, like Cushing’s syndrome or Addison’s disease, which are uncommon.

## What helps

The rhythm responds to rhythm: waking at a steady time, morning light, regular meals, movement and real breaks in the day.

## When to talk to your doctor

If you have unexplained weight changes, high blood pressure, easy bruising, dizziness when you stand, or deep fatigue that doesn’t lift with rest, see your doctor so they can check properly.
