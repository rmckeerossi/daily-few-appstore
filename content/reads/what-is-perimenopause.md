Menopause is a single point in time: 12 months in a row without a period. Perimenopause is the stretch of years leading up to it, and for many people it arrives earlier, and lasts longer, than they expected.

## When it starts

Perimenopause most often begins in your 40s, though some people notice changes in their late 30s. It lasts several years on average, and for some people closer to a decade. In the U.S., the average age of menopause is around 51.

## What’s happening

Your ovaries don’t wind down smoothly. Estrogen can swing higher and lower than before, month to month, while progesterone tends to fall as ovulation becomes less regular. Those swings, rather than a steady decline, explain a lot of the symptoms.

## What it can feel like

- Cycles that get shorter, longer, heavier or less predictable
- Hot flashes and night sweats
- Lighter, more broken sleep
- Mood changes, anxiety or irritability, sometimes new for you
- Brain fog and trouble finding words
- Joint aches, vaginal dryness and lower sex drive

Not everyone gets all of these, and many people don’t link them to hormones at first.

## You don’t have to wait it out

There are effective options, from changes to sleep, movement and routine to hormone therapy and non-hormonal medicines. The right choice depends on your symptoms and health history, and a doctor or menopause specialist can talk it through with you.

## When to talk to your doctor

If symptoms are affecting your sleep, work or relationships, make an appointment. See your doctor promptly for very heavy bleeding, bleeding between periods, or any bleeding after a full year without periods.
