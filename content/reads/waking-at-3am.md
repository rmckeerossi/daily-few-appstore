You fall asleep fine, then wake at 3 or 4am with your mind racing and can’t drift off again. It’s one of the most common sleep complaints, and it often has a few causes layered together.

## What might be behind it

- **Stress.** Cortisol, the hormone that helps you wake up, naturally starts rising in the early morning. When stress is high, sleep in those hours gets lighter and easier to break.
- **Alcohol.** A drink can help you fall asleep, but as it wears off, the second half of the night becomes lighter and more broken.
- **Hormone changes.** In the days before a period, and especially in perimenopause, falling estrogen and progesterone can make sleep more fragile. Night sweats can wake you too.
- **Eating patterns.** A very early dinner or a late, very sugary snack may play a part for some people, though the research here is less clear.
- **The simple things.** A full bladder, a restless partner, light or noise.

## What can help

- Keeping the same wake-up time, even after a bad night
- Not checking the clock or your phone, which tends to wake the mind up
- If you’ve been awake for what feels like 20 minutes, getting up to do something quiet in low light until you feel sleepy
- Noticing whether it lines up with your cycle, alcohol or stressful weeks

## When to talk to your doctor

If early waking happens most nights for a few weeks, or comes with low mood, loud snoring or gasping, or night sweats, mention it. Insomnia is very treatable, and one of the most effective approaches, cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I), doesn’t involve medication.
