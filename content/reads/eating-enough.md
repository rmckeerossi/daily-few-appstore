Most nutrition messages are about eating less. But a lot of women, especially busy or active ones, aren’t eating enough for what their bodies are doing, often without meaning to.

## How it happens

- Skipping breakfast and working through lunch
- Very active weeks without eating more to match
- Long-term dieting, or cutting out whole food groups
- Stress or a hectic stretch that dulls your appetite

## What your body does

When the energy coming in doesn’t cover what you’re using, your body protects itself by turning down things it can put on hold. Your cycle is one of them. Doctors call this low energy availability. The brain can lower its signals to the ovaries, and periods may become irregular or stop. This is called hypothalamic amenorrhea.

Other signs of under-fuelling can include:

- Tiredness, feeling cold, or getting ill more often
- Irritability, low mood and poor concentration
- Trouble sleeping
- Strong cravings, or thinking about food a lot
- Injuries that are slow to heal, like stress fractures

## Why it matters

Estrogen helps protect your bones and heart. Missing periods for a long time can lower bone density, so this is worth taking seriously, even when it feels like just a missed period.

## What can help

Regular meals and snacks, eating more on active days, and including carbohydrates, protein and fat. If you train a lot, a sports dietitian can help you match food to what you do.

## When to talk to your doctor

Talk to your doctor if you’ve missed three periods in a row, you’re not pregnant, and you’ve been very active, stressed or eating less. If food or your body feels hard to think about, it’s okay to ask for help with that too.
