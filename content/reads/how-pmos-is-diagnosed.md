Many people with PMOS (formerly PCOS) live with symptoms for years before anyone connects them. Knowing what doctors look for can help you ask the right questions.

## What doctors look for

In adults, a diagnosis usually needs at least two of these three:

- **Irregular or missing ovulation**, usually showing up as irregular, infrequent or absent periods
- **Signs of higher androgens**, either in blood tests or as symptoms like acne, extra hair on the face, chest or stomach, or thinning hair on the scalp
- **Many follicles on the ovaries** seen on ultrasound, or a high AMH level on a blood test

Other conditions that can look similar, like thyroid problems or high prolactin, are ruled out first.

## Why it gets missed

Symptoms vary a lot. One person has irregular cycles and no acne. Another has fairly regular cycles and noticeable hair growth. Hormonal birth control can also mask irregular periods and acne, so some signs only appear after stopping it.

## The metabolic side

Because PMOS is also a metabolic condition, the international guideline recommends checking blood sugar, cholesterol and blood pressure, and asking about mood and sleep, whatever your weight.

## Questions you might bring

- Could my irregular cycles and these other symptoms be connected?
- Which blood tests would help, and when in my cycle should I have them?
- Should my blood sugar be checked, and how often?
- What are my options if I’m not trying to get pregnant, and if I am?
