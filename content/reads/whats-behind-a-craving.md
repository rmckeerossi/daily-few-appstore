Cravings usually get talked about as something to resist. But a craving is information. It’s your body and brain telling you something, and it helps to know what that might be.

## Common reasons

- **You’re hungry.** Skipping meals or eating too little earlier in the day is one of the most common reasons for strong cravings later.
- **You’re tired.** Short sleep raises ghrelin, a hormone that drives hunger, and lowers leptin, which helps you feel full. Sweet, energy-dense foods get more appealing.
- **You’re stressed.** Stress hormones can increase appetite, and comforting foods genuinely help people feel calmer in the moment.
- **Your cycle.** Many people crave more in the week before their period, when the body uses a little more energy.
- **Your last meal was mostly quick carbohydrates.** It can leave you hungry again soon after.
- **Habit and emotion.** A food linked to comfort, reward or a time of day. That’s human, not a flaw.

## Working with cravings, not against them

- Eating regularly, with protein, fibre and some fat, so you’re not running on empty
- Letting yourself have the food you want in a way that feels good, rather than banning it, which tends to make cravings stronger
- Noticing patterns: time of day, cycle day, sleep and stress

## When to talk to your doctor

If cravings come with intense thirst, peeing a lot, or feeling unwell after eating, ask to have your blood sugar checked. Cravings for ice, clay or other non-food things can be a sign of low iron. And if eating feels out of control or causes you distress, support is available and it helps.
