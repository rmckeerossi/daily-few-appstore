Some anxiety has an obvious source. Other times it just appears: a racing heart, a tight chest, a sense of dread with no clear reason. When that happens, it helps to know your body can be part of the story.

## Physical things that can feed anxiety

- **Your cycle.** Some people feel more anxious or on edge in the week before their period, as estrogen and progesterone fall.
- **Perimenopause.** New anxiety in your late 30s or 40s is a common, and often overlooked, sign of hormone changes.
- **Sleep.** After a short night, the brain’s alarm system is more reactive.
- **Caffeine.** It can cause a racing heart and jitters that feel a lot like anxiety, especially on an empty stomach.
- **Long gaps without eating.** Feeling shaky and irritable when hungry can tip into anxiety for some people.
- **Thyroid.** An overactive thyroid can cause a fast heartbeat, restlessness and worry.
- **Alcohol.** The day after can bring a rebound of anxious feelings.

## This doesn’t make it “just hormones”

Anxiety is real whether or not it has a physical trigger, and it deserves care either way. Knowing the physical side simply gives you more ways to help it.

## What can help

- Noting when it shows up, alongside your cycle, sleep and caffeine
- Slow breathing with a longer breath out, which signals the body to settle
- Regular meals, and caffeine earlier in the day
- Talking to someone you trust

## When to talk to your doctor

If anxiety is frequent, gets in the way of daily life, or comes with panic attacks, talk to your doctor. Therapy and medication both help, and a thyroid check can rule out a physical cause. If you’re in crisis in the U.S., call or text 988, or contact your local emergency number.
