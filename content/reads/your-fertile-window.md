Whether you’re trying to get pregnant or just want to understand your cycle, the fertile window is worth knowing about. It’s shorter, and more specific, than many people assume.

## How it works

Once an egg is released at ovulation, it can be fertilized for about 12 to 24 hours. Sperm can survive in the body for up to 5 days. So the fertile window is about six days: the five days before ovulation and the day of ovulation itself. The two or three days just before ovulation are the most likely to lead to pregnancy.

## When ovulation happens

Ovulation usually happens about 14 days before your next period, not 14 days after your last one. In a 28-day cycle that’s around day 14. In a 32-day cycle, it’s closer to day 18.

Signs you might be near ovulation:

- Cervical mucus that’s clear, slippery and stretchy, like raw egg white
- A positive result on an ovulation predictor test
- A small rise in your resting temperature, which shows up just after ovulation
- A twinge of pain on one side, for some people

## When to ask for help

The general guidance is to see a doctor if you haven’t become pregnant after:

- 12 months of trying, if you’re under 35
- 6 months, if you’re 35 or older
- Sooner, if your cycles are irregular or absent, or you have a condition like PMOS (formerly PCOS) or endometriosis

Trying to conceive can be a lot to carry. It’s okay to ask for support early.
