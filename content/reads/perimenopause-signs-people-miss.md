When people picture menopause, they picture hot flashes. But the earliest signs of perimenopause often look nothing like that, which is why so many women don’t connect the dots for years.

## Signs that are easy to miss

- **Anxiety or low mood** that feels new, or stronger than before
- **Waking at night**, sometimes with a racing heart or feeling hot
- **Heavier or closer-together periods**, especially early on
- **Stronger premenstrual symptoms** than you used to have
- **Brain fog**, like losing words or forgetting why you walked into a room
- **Aches and stiffness** in your joints
- **Changes in skin and hair**, like dryness or thinning
- **Needing to pee more often**, or more urinary infections

## Why they get missed

Many of these have other possible causes, and they often arrive during a busy, demanding stage of life. It’s easy to put them down to stress, age or just a hard year.

Blood tests aren’t very reliable for confirming perimenopause either, because hormone levels change so much from day to day. For most people over 45, doctors go by age, cycle changes and symptoms.

## Why noticing it helps

Putting a name to what’s happening can be a relief. It also opens up options, because many of these symptoms respond well to treatment.

## When to talk to your doctor

If several of these sound familiar and you’re in your late 30s or 40s, it’s worth raising. A few months of notes on your cycle, sleep and mood give your doctor a clearer picture than any single test.
