Insulin usually comes up in conversations about diabetes. But it’s a hormone that affects far more than blood sugar, and it’s one reason symptoms that seem unrelated can share a root.

## What insulin does

After you eat, insulin helps move sugar from your blood into your cells, where it’s used for energy or stored. It also sends signals to the liver, to fat tissue and, importantly for women, to the ovaries.

## When cells stop listening

Sometimes cells respond less well to insulin. This is called insulin resistance. To keep blood sugar in range, the body makes more insulin. Blood sugar tests can look normal for years while insulin is working overtime.

Higher insulin can nudge the ovaries to make more androgens, hormones like testosterone. That’s one reason insulin resistance is closely linked with PMOS (formerly PCOS), irregular cycles, acne and extra hair growth.

It can also show up as:

- Energy dips and strong cravings, especially after carbohydrate-heavy meals
- More weight around the middle, even without changes in how you eat
- Darker, velvety patches of skin on the neck or in skin folds

## What helps

Muscles become more responsive to insulin with regular movement, including a walk after meals. Sleep helps too, and so do meals built around protein and fibre. Some people also benefit from medication, which is a conversation for your doctor.

## When to talk to your doctor

If several of these sound familiar, ask about checking blood sugar markers like fasting glucose and HbA1c. Insulin resistance is common, often silent, and much easier to work with when it’s found early.
