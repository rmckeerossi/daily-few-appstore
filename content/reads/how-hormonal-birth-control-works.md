Hormonal birth control uses versions of hormones your body already makes, estrogen and progestin (a form of progesterone), to prevent pregnancy. Different methods do this in slightly different ways.

## The main types

- **Combined methods** (the pill, patch and ring) contain estrogen and progestin. They mainly work by stopping ovulation.
- **Progestin-only pills** mainly thicken cervical mucus so sperm can’t get through, and some also stop ovulation.
- **The implant and the injection** mainly stop ovulation.
- **Hormonal IUDs** release a small amount of progestin inside the uterus. They thicken cervical mucus and thin the uterine lining. Many people still ovulate.

## Is a pill bleed a period?

Not quite. On combined methods, the bleed during the break week is a withdrawal bleed, caused by the drop in hormones rather than a natural cycle. That’s why it’s often lighter, and why many people can skip the break if their doctor agrees.

## What it can and can’t tell you

Because many methods pause ovulation, your bleeding pattern on birth control doesn’t tell you much about your natural cycle. That isn’t good or bad. It’s just useful to know if you’re trying to understand your body.

## Beyond contraception

Hormonal methods are also used to manage heavy or painful periods, endometriosis, acne and some symptoms of PMOS (formerly PCOS).

## Finding the right fit

The best method is the one that fits your body, your health history and your life. Your doctor can help you weigh the options, and it’s fine to change your mind.
