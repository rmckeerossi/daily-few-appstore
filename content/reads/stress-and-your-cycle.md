If your period has ever arrived late during a hard month, that’s not a coincidence. The parts of the brain that manage stress also help run your cycle.

## What’s going on

Your cycle is directed by the brain. The hypothalamus and pituitary gland send signals to the ovaries that lead to ovulation. The same area of the brain sits at the centre of your stress response, sometimes called the HPA axis (hypothalamus, pituitary and adrenal glands).

When stress is intense or goes on for a long time, the brain can turn down the signals to the ovaries. Ovulation may come later, or not happen that month. Because your period follows ovulation, it can arrive late or be skipped.

Stress can also show up as:

- Stronger premenstrual symptoms
- Periods that are heavier, lighter or more painful than usual
- Spotting between periods

## Stress isn’t only emotional

Your body counts many kinds of load as stress: poor sleep, illness, travel, intense training and not eating enough. They often arrive together, which is why cycles tend to shift during big life moments.

## What can help

Noting your cycle alongside how your weeks felt can show whether stress is a pattern for you. Sleep, regular meals and anything that genuinely lowers the pressure all help the signals settle.

## When to talk to your doctor

One late period in a stressful month is common. Talk to your doctor if you’ve missed three periods in a row, if your cycle has changed and stayed changed, or if there’s any chance you’re pregnant. Irregular cycles can have other causes, like thyroid problems or PMOS (formerly PCOS), and they’re worth checking.
