A lot of us grew up believing a cycle should be exactly 28 days. In reality, 28 is just an average, and plenty of healthy cycles look different.

## What’s typical for adults

- **Cycle length:** counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next, usually anywhere from 21 to 35 days
- **Variation:** a few days’ difference from month to month is normal
- **Bleeding:** usually lasts 2 to 7 days, heaviest in the first day or two

In the first few years after periods start, and again in perimenopause, cycles are often less predictable.

## What your cycle can tell you

A regular cycle is a sign that ovulation is usually happening. That depends on your brain, ovaries, thyroid, metabolism and stress levels all working together, which is why a change in your cycle is sometimes the first sign that something else has shifted.

## Worth mentioning to your doctor

- Cycles shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days
- Going 3 months without a period when you’re not pregnant
- Bleeding that soaks a pad or tampon every hour or two, or lasts more than 7 days
- Bleeding between periods or after sex
- Periods painful enough to stop you doing normal things
- A pattern that has changed and stayed changed

Keeping a simple record, like the dates of your periods and how you felt, makes that conversation much easier.
