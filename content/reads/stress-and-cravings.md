On the most stretched days, it’s common to want something sweet, salty or comforting more than usual. If you’ve ever blamed yourself for that, here’s the other side of the story.

## What’s going on

When you’re under stress, your body releases stress hormones, including cortisol. In the short term, they help you respond to whatever is asking a lot of you. When stress keeps going, cortisol can stay raised, and that’s linked with a bigger appetite and a stronger pull toward high-sugar and high-fat foods.

Foods like these can also feel soothing in the moment, which is part of why they’re so appealing on hard days. Tiredness adds to it: short sleep is linked with stronger hunger signals too.

So stronger cravings on stressful days are a very human response, not a lack of discipline.

## What tends to help

- Noticing the pattern, which your check-ins can show you
- Regular meals, so you’re not running on empty when stress peaks
- Small stress releases through the day: a walk, a few slow breaths, stepping outside
- Protecting sleep where you can

## When to talk to your doctor

If stress feels constant, if eating feels out of your control, or if big changes in appetite come without explanation, mention it to your doctor. You don’t have to have it all figured out first.
