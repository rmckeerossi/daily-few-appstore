You drag through the day on coffee, then get into bed and your brain lights up. Being exhausted and unable to rest at the same time feels contradictory, but it makes sense once you look at what stress does to the body.

## What’s going on

Under pressure, your body releases stress hormones like cortisol and adrenaline. They’re useful: they sharpen focus and give you energy to get through a demanding day. The trouble comes when the pressure doesn’t let up. Your body stays in a more alert mode, even when you finally stop.

A few things often keep the loop going:

- Caffeine later in the day, which can stay in your system for hours
- Screens and work late in the evening, which keep your mind in doing mode
- Going to bed at very different times from night to night
- No real pause during the day, so bedtime is the first chance your thoughts get

## Why you still feel tired

Poor sleep leaves you tired the next day, so you lean harder on caffeine and adrenaline, which makes the next night harder. It’s a loop, not a personal failing.

## What can help

- A caffeine cut-off, around eight hours before bed
- A wind-down time, even 20 minutes, with dim light and no work
- Morning daylight, which helps set your body clock
- Small pauses during the day, so stress has somewhere to go before bedtime

## When to talk to your doctor

If exhaustion lasts for weeks despite rest, or comes with low mood, a racing heart, or always feeling too hot or too cold, ask for a check-up. Thyroid problems, low iron and depression can all feel like this, and they’re worth ruling out.
