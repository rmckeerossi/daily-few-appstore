Hormonal birth control is one of the most studied medicines there is, and it works well for millions of people. It’s still normal to notice changes when you start, switch or stop, and knowing what to expect takes out some of the guesswork.

## When you start or switch

The first 2 to 3 months are an adjustment period. Common, usually temporary, changes include:

- Spotting or irregular bleeding
- Nausea, headaches or breast tenderness
- Changes in your skin, for better or worse
- Mood changes, for some people

If side effects don’t settle after about three months, or they’re affecting your life, talk to your doctor. There are many types and doses, and switching often helps.

## When you stop

With most methods, your natural cycle comes back within a few months. It can take longer after the injection.

A few things can surprise people:

- **Your first natural periods may be irregular** for a little while.
- **Symptoms from before may return.** Birth control is often used to manage acne or heavy, painful periods, so those can come back when you stop.
- **Some conditions show up for the first time.** Because hormonal birth control regulates bleeding, it can mask irregular cycles from conditions like PMOS (formerly PCOS). The birth control didn’t cause them. They were there underneath.

## When to talk to your doctor

If your period hasn’t come back three months after stopping, and you’re not pregnant, get it checked. If you’re stopping because you want to get pregnant, it’s a good time to ask about folic acid and a preconception check-up.

With methods that contain estrogen, get medical help straight away for a severe headache, chest pain, leg pain or swelling, or sudden changes in your vision.
