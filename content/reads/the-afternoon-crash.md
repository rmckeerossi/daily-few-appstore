Mid-afternoon, focus slips, your eyelids get heavy, and suddenly the snack drawer is all you can think about. Almost everyone knows this slump. It usually has more than one cause.

## What’s going on

Part of it is your body clock. Alertness naturally dips in the early afternoon, even on days you skip lunch. It’s built into the same daily rhythm that makes you sleepy at night.

Lunch can add to it. After a meal, blood sugar rises and the hormone insulin helps move that sugar into your cells. A meal that’s mostly quick-digesting carbohydrate, like white bread, pastries or a sweet drink on its own, tends to raise blood sugar quickly and let it fall quickly. Some people feel that fall as tiredness, shakiness or a sudden craving.

Short sleep makes all of it stronger. After a rough night, the afternoon dip hits harder and sweet foods get more appealing.

## Things that often help

- Pairing carbohydrates with protein, fibre or fat, which slows how quickly they’re absorbed
- Eating lunch at all, rather than running on coffee until mid-afternoon
- A short walk or some daylight after eating
- Protecting sleep where you can

None of this is about cutting foods out. It’s about giving your body something steady to run on.

## When to talk to your doctor

If you’re tired most of the day, very thirsty, peeing more than usual, or feel shaky and unwell a few hours after eating, mention it. Iron deficiency, thyroid changes and blood sugar problems can all feel like this, and simple blood tests can help.
