When people think about hormones, they usually think about the ovaries or the thyroid. But your gut plays a part too, in how hormones are cleared once they’ve done their job.

## How it works

Once estrogen has been used, the liver packages it up so it can leave the body. Some leaves in urine. Some is sent into the gut in bile, to leave with your stool.

In the gut, certain bacteria make an enzyme that can unpack some of that estrogen, letting it be absorbed back into the blood. Researchers call the gut bacteria that affect estrogen in this way the estrobolome.

## What’s known and what isn’t

This research is new and moving quickly. Studies suggest the make-up of your gut bacteria may influence estrogen levels, and researchers are looking at links with conditions like endometriosis and PMOS (formerly PCOS). But there isn’t yet a test or product that can reliably tell you your estrobolome is off, or fix it. Be wary of anyone who says otherwise.

## What’s good for your gut anyway

- **Fibre**, from vegetables, fruit, beans, lentils and whole grains, which feeds helpful bacteria and keeps things moving
- **Regular bowel movements.** With constipation, waste, including used hormones, stays in the gut longer.
- **Variety.** A wider range of plant foods supports a wider range of bacteria.
- **Fermented foods**, like yogurt or kefir, if you enjoy them

## When to talk to your doctor

See your doctor for ongoing changes in your bowel habits, blood in your stool, unexplained weight loss, or stomach pain that doesn’t go away.
