Feeling puffy, tight or heavy in your stomach is one of the most common complaints there is. Often it’s linked to food or your cycle. Sometimes it’s worth a closer look.

## Why it happens around your period

- **Progesterone**, which rises after ovulation, can slow digestion, so things move more slowly and gas builds up.
- **Fluid retention** is common in the days before a period.
- **Prostaglandins**, released as your period starts, can speed the gut up, which is why some people get looser stools on day one.

## Other everyday causes

- Constipation
- Eating quickly, fizzy drinks or swallowing air
- Foods that ferment in the gut, like beans, onions and some sweeteners, especially if you’re sensitive to them
- Stress, which changes how the gut moves
- Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), which is common in women and can flare around periods

## What can help

- Noticing whether bloating follows your cycle, certain foods or stressful days
- Eating more slowly, and moving after meals
- More fibre and water, increased gradually, to help with constipation

## When to get it checked

See your doctor if bloating:

- Happens most days for three weeks or more
- Comes with feeling full quickly, loss of appetite, pelvic or stomach pain, or needing to pee more often
- Comes with unexplained weight loss, blood in your stool or changes in your bowel habits

These usually have causes that aren’t serious, but persistent bloating is something doctors want to hear about, because occasionally it can be an early sign of ovarian cancer.
