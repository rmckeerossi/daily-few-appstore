There’s a lot of talk about getting your body back after a baby. What gets less attention is how much is still shifting inside, for months, not weeks.

## Hormones after birth

In pregnancy, estrogen and progesterone reach very high levels. Within days of birth they drop sharply. That fall, along with exhaustion, is part of why so many people feel tearful and overwhelmed in the first couple of weeks, often called the baby blues.

If you’re breastfeeding, the hormone prolactin stays high and estrogen stays low. That can mean vaginal dryness, lower sex drive and periods that stay away for months.

## Other changes that are common

- Periods returning anywhere from a few weeks to many months after birth, and sometimes feeling different
- Hair shedding around 3 to 4 months after birth
- Night sweats in the early weeks
- Changes in your pelvic floor, like leaking when you cough or laugh

## Worth checking with your doctor

- **Low mood or anxiety lasting more than 2 weeks**, or thoughts of harming yourself or your baby. Postpartum depression and anxiety are common and treatable. In the U.S., you can call or text the National Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262, or 988 in a crisis.
- **Feeling racing, shaky or unusually exhausted** in the months after birth, which can be a sign of postpartum thyroiditis
- **Leaking or heaviness in your pelvis** that isn’t improving, which pelvic floor physical therapy can help with

## Go gently

Your body did something enormous. Recovery isn’t a deadline, and getting back to baseline looks different for everyone.
