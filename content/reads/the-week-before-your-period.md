If the days before your period feel heavier, slower or shorter-fused, you’re not imagining it, and you’re far from alone. Many people who menstruate notice changes in the week or two before a period.

## What’s changing

A cycle has two halves. After ovulation, the body makes more progesterone. If there’s no pregnancy, progesterone and estrogen both fall in the days before your period starts.

Those shifting hormone levels can affect how you feel. Common changes include:

- Lower energy or feeling more tired than usual
- Mood changes, like irritability, sadness or feeling tense
- Sleep that’s harder to settle into, or less restful
- Stronger cravings, often for sweet or salty foods
- Bloating or tender breasts

This is often called premenstrual syndrome, or PMS. How much it shows up varies a lot from person to person, and from cycle to cycle.

## Why noticing it helps

Seeing the pattern can take some of the sting out of it. A hard Tuesday feels different when you know it tends to land in the same part of your cycle. It also helps you plan: lighter days where you can, more rest, fewer big decisions.

## When to talk to your doctor

If premenstrual changes regularly get in the way of work, relationships or daily life, or if your mood drops very low, tell your doctor. A small number of people have a more severe form called premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD), and there are treatments that help. Bringing a few months of check-ins can make that conversation easier.
