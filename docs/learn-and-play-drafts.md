## 1. Fact or fiction

Swipe right for fact, left for fiction. Instant feedback, one line of truth, and a read to go deeper where there is one.

1. **"A normal cycle is exactly 28 days."**
   **Fiction.** Anywhere from about 21 to 35 days is typical for adults, and a few days’ difference from month to month is normal.
   Read: What counts as a regular cycle

2. **"You lose about a cup of blood during a period."**
   **Fiction.** It’s usually closer to two or three tablespoons. If you’re soaking through a pad or tampon every hour or two, that’s worth mentioning to your doctor.
   Read: What counts as a regular cycle

3. **"Stress can make your period late."**
   **Fact.** The part of the brain that handles stress also helps run your cycle, so a hard month can delay ovulation, and your period with it.
   Read: How stress can show up in your cycle

4. **"Pre-period cravings are all in your head."**
   **Fiction.** Cravings in the days before a period are common and real. Sleep, stress and shifting hormones all play a part.
   Read: What’s actually behind a craving

5. **"PMOS (formerly PCOS) only affects women in larger bodies."**
   **Fiction.** It affects women of every body size, and it’s often missed in women who don’t fit that picture.
   Read: How PMOS is diagnosed

6. **"Perimenopause can start in your late 30s."**
   **Fact.** It most often begins in your 40s, but some people notice changes earlier, and it can last for several years.
   Read: Perimenopause: the years no one warned you about

7. **"A bad night’s sleep can make you hungrier the next day."**
   **Fact.** Short sleep nudges up the hormone that drives hunger and turns down the one that helps you feel full.
   Read: Why one rough night can colour the whole next day

8. **"Cortisol is a bad hormone."**
   **Fiction.** You couldn’t get through a day without it. It follows a daily rhythm, and it’s when that rhythm gets thrown off that you feel it.
   Read: What cortisol actually does

9. **"Your gut helps your body get rid of used estrogen."**
   **Fact.** Your liver packages it up and much of it leaves through the gut, which is one reason regular digestion matters.
   Read: How your gut helps clear hormones

10. **"Irregular periods are just something you have to live with."**
    **Fiction.** They often have a cause worth looking into, like thyroid changes, stress or PMOS (formerly PCOS). It’s worth raising with your doctor.
    Read: What counts as a regular cycle

11. **"Moving your body can ease period cramps for some people."**
    **Fact.** Gentle movement helps some people feel better. It isn’t a cure, and painful periods that stop you doing normal things are worth checking.
    Read: Why the week before your period can feel so different

12. **"You can’t get pregnant on your period."**
    **Fiction.** It’s less likely, but possible, especially with shorter cycles, because sperm can survive for up to five days.
    Read: Your fertile window, simply explained

## 2. Around the world

One tradition a day from a different place, with a small, gentle way to try it tonight. Nothing medical, nothing to swallow, nothing that needs buying.

1. **Fika · Sweden**
   A real pause in the day for coffee or tea and something sweet, ideally with someone else. Not at your desk, and not rushed.
   *Try it tonight:* make a hot drink, sit down properly, and put your phone in another room for ten minutes.

2. **Hygge · Denmark**
   The feeling of cosiness and comfort, often shared: low light, warm drinks, good company.
   *Try it tonight:* switch off the overhead light, turn on a lamp or a candle, and get under a blanket.

3. **Shinrin-yoku · Japan**
   "Forest bathing": slow, quiet time among trees, noticing rather than exercising.
   *Try it tonight or tomorrow:* walk somewhere green for ten minutes, slowly, and notice five things you can hear.

4. **Niksen · The Netherlands**
   The art of doing nothing on purpose. Not scrolling, not planning, just letting your mind wander.
   *Try it tonight:* sit by a window for five minutes and do absolutely nothing.

5. **Lagom · Sweden**
   "Just the right amount": not too much, not too little.
   *Try it tonight:* pick one thing you usually overdo, and do just enough of it.

6. **Friluftsliv · Norway**
   "Open-air life": time outdoors in all weather, as part of everyday life.
   *Try it tonight:* step outside for five minutes before bed and look up.

7. **Kintsugi · Japan**
   Repairing broken pottery with gold, so the cracks become part of the beauty.
   *Try it tonight:* write down one thing you’ve come through that left you stronger.

8. **Ubuntu · Southern Africa**
   "I am because we are": the idea that we become who we are through each other.
   *Try it tonight:* send one message thanking someone who shaped you.

9. **Hammam · Morocco**
   A weekly ritual of warmth, steam and slow care, often shared with other women.
   *Try it tonight:* take a long, warm shower with no rushing, and moisturise slowly afterwards.

10. **Riposo · Italy**
    A slower stretch of the day after lunch, when shops close and people rest.
    *Try it this weekend:* after lunch, lie down for fifteen minutes with no alarm and no screen.

11. **Zuo yuezi · China**
    "Sitting the month": a time of rest and care for new mothers after birth, often with family stepping in to help.
    *Try it tonight:* offer, or ask for, one act of care.

12. **Tea ceremony · Japan**
    Making and sharing tea slowly and with attention, so an ordinary act becomes a ritual.
    *Try it tonight:* make one cup of tea with your full attention, start to finish.

## 3. Weird history

Short, surprising and true, with a quiet point: for centuries, women’s bodies were misunderstood by the system, not failed by the women in them.

1. **The wandering womb**
   Ancient Greek medical writers believed the womb could wander around the body, causing everything from fainting to breathlessness. The word "hysteria" comes from the Greek for womb.
   *Source:* Hippocratic writings, around 400 BCE.

2. **"A cup of wine and don’t go near the crops"**
   The Roman writer Pliny the Elder claimed a woman on her period could sour wine, dull mirrors and make crops wither.
   *Source:* Pliny the Elder, *Natural History*, 1st century CE.

3. **The world’s first pregnancy test**
   Ancient Egyptians tested for pregnancy by having a woman urinate on barley and wheat seeds. If they sprouted, she was likely pregnant. In the 1960s, researchers tried it and found it often worked.
   *Source:* Berlin Medical Papyrus (around 1300 BCE); Ghalioungui and colleagues, 1963.

4. **The rest cure**
   In the late 1800s, women with "nervous" symptoms were often prescribed weeks of total bed rest, with no reading, writing or visitors. Charlotte Perkins Gilman wrote *The Yellow Wallpaper* about her experience of it.
   *Source:* S. Weir Mitchell’s rest cure; Gilman, 1892.

5. **Hysteria stayed a diagnosis until 1980**
   "Hysterical neurosis" only disappeared from the main American psychiatric manual in 1980.
   *Source:* DSM-III, American Psychiatric Association, 1980.

6. **Women were left out of drug trials**
   In 1977, US guidance recommended excluding women who could become pregnant from early drug trials. It wasn’t reversed until 1993, so many medicines were first studied mostly in men.
   *Source:* FDA guidance 1977, reversed 1993; NIH Revitalization Act, 1993.

7. **Pads began as bandages**
   During the First World War, nurses noticed that wood-pulp bandages made for soldiers worked well as period pads. One of the first disposable pads went on sale shortly after.
   *Source:* Kotex, launched 1920.

8. **PMS got its name in 1953**
   Doctors Katharina Dalton and Raymond Greene coined "premenstrual syndrome" in a 1953 paper, giving a name to something women had been describing for centuries.
   *Source:* Greene and Dalton, *British Medical Journal*, 1953.

9. **Menopause was sold as a disease**
   A 1966 bestseller called *Feminine Forever* described menopause as a deficiency to be cured, shaping how a generation saw a natural stage of life.
   *Source:* Robert A. Wilson, *Feminine Forever*, 1966.

10. **The pill arrived in 1960**
    The first birth control pill was approved in the US in 1960. Within a few years, millions of women were taking it.
    *Source:* FDA approval of Enovid, 1960.

11. **"Premenstrual tension" was described in 1931**
    An American gynaecologist described the tension some women feel before their period more than twenty years before "PMS" had a name.
    *Source:* Robert T. Frank, 1931.

12. **The womb was blamed for almost everything**
    For centuries, doctors linked headaches, sadness, fatigue and even sneezing to the womb, which is part of why women’s symptoms were so often brushed aside.
    *Source:* Historical medical texts, from ancient Greece to the 19th century.
